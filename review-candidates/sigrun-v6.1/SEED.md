\[

(\mathcal S,\mathcal O,\mathcal B,\mathcal I,\mathcal D,\mathcal W,

\mathcal G,\Pi,\mathcal V,\mathcal A,\mathcal F)

with:

- `S`: possible world states;

- `I`: operator intent, utility and protected effect boundaries;
- `D`: durable virtual actors;

where:

- `Lambda_t` is current lease/fencing/idempotency state;

# 4. Partial observability and zero-trust belief

## 4.1 World state is not directly available

The true world state `s_t in S` is only partially observed:

\[
o_t \sim Z(\cdot\mid s_t)
\]

HFO maintains a belief state. Because evidence can be incomplete or contradictory, the safest object is often a **credal set** rather than one precise probability distribution:

\[
B_t \subseteq \Delta(\mathcal S)
\]

A large/non-singleton `B_t` represents explicit uncertainty.

## 4.2 Evidence items

An evidence item is:

\[
e=(\text{claim},\text{value},\text{source},\text{grade},t_v,t_\tau,\text{provenance})
\]

where:

- `t_v` is valid time;
- `t_tau` is transaction/observation time.

## 4.3 Evidence precedence is a partial order

HFO uses an evidence order `preceq_E`, approximately:

\[
\text{chat}
\prec
\text{Slack/discussion}
\prec
\text{checkpoint}
\prec
\text{current projection}
\prec
\text{independent RESULT/VERDICT/ACT}
\prec
\text{exact provider/world/byte readback}
\]

This is not the claim that every higher source is always semantically correct. It means a lower projection may not silently override a newer exact higher-grade observation.

## 4.4 Belief update

Belief revision is:

\[
B_{t+1}=\mathsf{Update}(B_t,O_{t+1},E_{t+1},\preceq_E)
\]

When evidence is incompatible and no admitted reducer can resolve it:

\[
B_{t+1}=\text{UNKNOWN or a non-singleton hypothesis set}
\]

not a convenient invented scalar truth.

# 6. Durable virtual actors and disposable carriers

## 6.1 Actor definition

A durable actor `d_i` is:

\[
d_i=(id_i,\gamma_i,\rho_i,\alpha_i,m_i,h_i,r_i,q_i)

where:

- `id_i`: stable actor identity;
- `gamma_i`: inherited germline;

- `alpha_i`: authority ceiling;
- `m_i`: memory pointers;
- `h_i`: accepted history/state cursor;
- `r_i`: reputation/evaluation state;
- `q_i`: current WorkItem references.

The actor is not the model.

## 6.2 Activation definition

An activation is:

\[
\phi_{i,k}=
\mathsf{Activate}
(d_i,w,\ell,c,x,\lambda,\kappa)
\]

where:

- `w` is the exact WorkItem;
- `ell` is the finite loadout;
- `c` is the disposable model/session/process carrier;
- `x` is the execution substrate;
- `lambda` is the lease/fencing identity;
- `kappa` is the exact ContextPack/MemoryBundle.

## 6.3 Carrier independence

For successive activations:

\[
c_k\neq c_{k+1}
\]

is allowed while:

\[
\mathsf{ActorId}(\phi_{i,k})=
\mathsf{ActorId}(\phi_{i,k+1})=id_i

provided the accepted state chain is continuous.

Identity continuity is observational and receipt-based, not a claim about hidden subjective continuity:

\[
\phi_{i,k}\sim_i\phi_{i,k+1}
\iff
\begin{cases}
\text{same actor id}\cr
\text{same admitted germline/role}\cr
\text{successor consumes exact accepted predecessor state}\cr
\text{authority does not silently widen}
\end{cases}
\]

## 9.1 WorkItem type

A WorkItem is:

\[
w=(job,id,phase,state,input,key,route,owner,effect,accept,next,retry,deadline)

It defines one bounded legal transition and its acceptance contract.

## 9.2 Eligibility

A WorkItem is eligible iff:

\[
\mathsf{eligible}(w,t)=
[state(w)=READY]
\land
[dependencies(w)\;satisfied]
\land
[capacity(w)\;available]
\land
[WIP\;limit\;allows]
\]

If there is no eligible work:

\[
Q_t^{eligible}=\varnothing
\Rightarrow
N_{\text{new workers}}=N_{\text{model requests}}=0
\]

This is the formal prevention of false metabolism.

## 9.3 Pull selection

A basic deterministic pull policy is:

\[
w^*=\arg\max_{w\in Q_t^{eligible}}
(priority(w),-ready\_time(w))
\]

subject to role/capability compatibility.

## 9.4 Lease/fence invariant

For an idempotency key `k`:

\[
\#\{\lambda:\lambda.key=k\land\lambda.active\}\le1
\]

and:

\[
\#\{e:e.key=k\land e.accepted\}\le1
\]

The realistic target is not magical exactly-once physical execution. It is:

- at-most-one accepted canonical effect;
- idempotent or compensated external effects;
- stale fence losers perform zero accepted effect.

# 10. Candidate generation, verification and acceptance

## 10.1 Candidate generation

A stochastic worker produces:

\[
y\sim K_{c,\ell}(\cdot\mid w,\kappa)
\]

The output is only a candidate:

\[
y\in\widetilde{\mathcal Y}
\]

not canonical state.

## 10.2 Verification vector

Let verification be a vector:

\[
V(y,w)=
(V_{mechanical},V_{semantic},V_{authority},V_{provider},V_{human},V_{economic})
\]

The required subset depends on consequence class.

## 10.3 Acceptance

Canonical admission is:

\[
\mathsf{Admit}(y,w)=1
\iff
\mathsf{HardGates}(y,w)
\land
\mathsf{RequiredVerification}(y,w)
\land
\mathsf{Readback}(y,w)
\]

No receipt means no accepted transition:

\[
\neg\mathsf{Receipt}(y)\Rightarrow\mathsf{Admit}(y,w)=0
\]

## 10.4 Producer/verifier separation

For consequential work:

\[
producer(y)\neq verifier(y)
\]

A producer may run self-checks, but may not grant final promotion to itself.

## 10.5 Evidence-path independence

Let `P_y` be the provenance DAG for a verdict. A minimally independent verdict requires at least one evidence path from source/provider/mechanical observation to verdict that is not controlled by the producer:

\[
\exists p:\;source\leadsto verdict
\quad\text{such that}\quad producer\notin p
\]

Model-family plurality without independent evidence can remain correlated and is not world truth.
